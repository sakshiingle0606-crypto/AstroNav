Safe routes from the base

For each trip, Astronav compares the direct straight line with a safe path found by searching the terrain for the gentlest way through. Each cell below shows the verdict, then distance · steepest slope · total climbing.

RouteHowDirect lineSafe routeWhat the safe route changes
Worth knowing
  • Verdict uses the limits above: easy under 10°, medium 10° to 20°, hard over 20°.
  • Total climbing adds up every uphill step along the line, so small bumps in the data count too.
  • Safe paths are found by searching 40 m cells, avoiding cells steeper than 20° and favouring flatter ground. All three safe paths stay below 20°.
  • The terrain data cannot show boulders or rock formations, so the safe path avoids steep ground only. Rock hazards need higher-resolution imagery.
  • The distance a suited astronaut can walk is not modelled here, so long trips are shown as rover-assisted.

Worth Knowing
  • Reference values. Mars surface gravity is 0.376 g, about 3.71 m/s² (Universe Today). NASA's lunar surface suit requirements, which are for the Moon and not Mars, call for walking up, down and across slopes of up to 20°, spacewalks of up to 8 hours (6 ± 2 hours), and traverses of up to 2 km from the lander at about 2 km/h on level ground (NASA NTRS).
  • Assumptions. A result of "Fits" needs at least 2 hours left on site for sampling. Walking time ignores slowdown from slope, suit and equipment load, and Mars has no approved suit standard yet, so the lunar values are a reference only.

Method

How the map is made, and its limits

How did we map a place no humans have ever walked?

Spoiler

Method

  • Elevation comes from a 20 m terrain model of Jezero built from Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter CTX images.
  • Slope is the steepness between neighbouring 20 m cells. Relief shading is lit from the north-west to make the shape of the land readable.
  • Each cell is coloured easy, harder or very steep using the 10° and 20° limits.
  • Route and path distances use the Mars radius (3,396 km), about 59.3 km per degree of latitude.
Know our limits!!

Limits

  • With 20 m cells, boulders and small cliffs cannot be seen. Slope stands in for hazards and does not guarantee safety.
  • The 10° "easy" limit and the choice of example routes are planning assumptions. The 20° limit follows NASA's lunar suit requirement, which is for the Moon and not Mars.
  • Places marked "Approximate" are estimated from imagery. Only "Official" places use published coordinates.
  • This is a planning aid for study and demonstration, not a flight tool.

Reading the Martian landscape

Elevation comes from Calef’s (2021) Mars 2020 Science Investigation CTX DEM Mosaic, produced by USGS Astrogeology and the NASA Planetary Data System from Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter CTX images. Slope and relief shading are derived from this public-domain model.
